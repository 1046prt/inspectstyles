The questions that come up on every handoff, answered by pointing at the page.

Fonts, as rendered

Family, size in px and rem, weight with its name, line height as a ratio, and a live specimen set in the page's own colors.

The real background is worked out by walking up the page and blending transparent layers. You get the ratio and a pass or fail for AA and AAA.

Spacing at a glance

Margin, border and padding are drawn over the page and laid out as a box model in the panel, so layout questions get answered in one look.

The whole page, ranked

Scan once to see every font with its weights and sizes, plus the text and background colors in order of use. Useful for audits and for matching a brand.

Eyedropper and palette

Pick any pixel on screen, including images and gradients. Colors are copied and saved to a palette you can export as CSS variables.

:root {
  --color-1: #c4491c;
  --color-2: #f7f1e8;
}

Copy it your way

One click gives you clean CSS, Tailwind classes or JSON for the locked element. Colors switch between HEX, RGB and HSL.

font-['Arial'] text-[16px] font-[700]
text-[#ffffff] bg-[#c4491c]
rounded-[10px] py-[12px] px-[24px]

Questions

Short answers about permissions and privacy.

What does it send to a server?

Nothing. There are no network requests. Styles are read with the browser's own getComputedStyle, and your saved palette and settings live in chrome.storage on your device.

Why does it need the permissions it asks for?

activeTab and scripting let it run on the tab you choose, only when you start it. storage keeps your palette, theme and panel width.

Why does the eyedropper not work for me?

It uses the browser's EyeDropper feature, which needs Chrome or Edge 95 or newer. Firefox and Safari don't offer it.

Why can't I inspect a chrome:// page?

Browsers block extensions on their own pages and on the Chrome Web Store. Any regular website works.

Is the contrast ratio always exact?

It's exact for solid and semi-transparent colors. When a background image sits behind the text, the panel tells you the ratio is an estimate.
